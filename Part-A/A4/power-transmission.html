1.1 Introduction to Gears

  • A gear is a toothed machine element used to transmit rotary motion and power from one shaft to another.
  • Two gears transmit motion by the meshing of their teeth.
  • Unlike belt drives, gears provide a positive drive without slip under normal operating conditions.
  • Gears are used when accurate speed ratios and reliable power transmission are required.

Applications

  • Marine reduction gearboxes.
  • Diesel engine timing mechanisms.
  • Pumps and compressors.
  • Gear pumps.
  • Winches and cranes.
  • Industrial machinery.

Where D = pitch diameter (mm), Z = number of teeth, m = module (mm).

Important: Excessive backlash causes noise, vibration and inaccurate motion. Backlash allows space for lubrication and thermal expansion.

1.3 Classification of Gears

A. Parallel Shaft Gears

1. Spur Gears

  • Teeth are straight and parallel to the axis of rotation.
  • Used for transmitting power between parallel shafts.
  • Teeth engage suddenly along their width.
  • Simple construction, easy to manufacture.
  • More noise and vibration at high speeds compared with helical gears.
  • Generate no axial thrust under ideal conditions.
AdvantagesDisadvantages
Simple constructionNoisy at high speeds
High efficiencySudden tooth engagement
Easy maintenanceNot ideal for high-speed, quiet operation
Economical manufacturing—

2. Helical Gears

  • Teeth are cut at an angle to the shaft axis.
  • Tooth engagement takes place gradually.
  • More than one pair of teeth may be in contact simultaneously.
  • Operate more smoothly and quietly than spur gears.
  • Produce axial thrust due to the inclined teeth.
AdvantagesDisadvantages
Smooth and quiet operationAxial thrust is generated
Suitable for high-speed transmissionRequires suitable thrust bearings
Higher load-carrying capacityManufacturing more complex
—Power lost due to increased sliding friction

3. Double Helical / Herringbone Gears

  • Two sets of helical teeth arranged in opposite directions.
  • Axial thrust produced by one helix is balanced by the opposite helix.
  • Suitable for transmitting large amounts of power.
  • Smooth and quiet operation.

B. Intersecting Shaft Gears

4. Bevel Gears

  • Used to transmit power between intersecting shafts.
  • Teeth are formed on conical surfaces.
  • Commonly used when shaft axes intersect at 90°.
  • Straight bevel: straight teeth, simple, can be noisy at high speed.
  • Spiral bevel: curved teeth, smoother and quieter, suitable for higher speeds and heavier loads.

C. Non-Parallel and Non-Intersecting Shaft Gears

5. Worm and Worm Wheel

  • Screw-like component (worm) drives a toothed wheel (worm wheel).
  • Used between non-parallel and non-intersecting shafts.
  • Usually used for large speed reduction.
  • High reduction ratios in a compact arrangement.
  • Sliding contact produces considerable friction and heat — proper lubrication required.
  • Self-locking: certain arrangements prevent the worm wheel from driving the worm. Depends on lead angle and friction. Should not automatically be assumed in safety-critical lifting systems.

6. Crossed Helical Gears

  • For non-parallel, non-intersecting shafts.
  • Point or limited contact compared with parallel-axis helical gears.
  • Suitable mainly for light loads.

7. Hypoid Gears

  • Similar to spiral bevel gears, but shaft axes do not intersect.
  • Commonly used in automotive rear axle drives.

1.4 Gear Ratio and Speed Ratio

For two externally meshing gears:

N1N2 = Z2Z1

Where N₁ = driving gear speed, N₂ = driven gear speed, Z₁ = driving teeth, Z₂ = driven teeth.

Important observations

  • Smaller driving gear → larger driven gear reduces output speed.
  • Larger driving gear → smaller driven gear increases output speed.
  • External gears rotate in opposite directions.

1.5 Gear Trains

  • A gear train is a combination of two or more gears used to transmit motion and power.
  • Used when a large speed reduction or increase is required.
  • Can change direction and arrangement of rotating shafts.

1. Simple Gear Train

  • Each shaft carries only one gear.
  • Intermediate gears are called idler gears.
  • Idlers change direction of rotation or distance between gears.
  • Idlers do not change the overall speed ratio.
N1Nn = ZnZ1

2. Compound Gear Train

  • At least one shaft carries two gears rigidly fixed together.
  • Both gears on the same shaft rotate at the same speed.
  • Used to obtain large speed reduction in a compact arrangement.
N1N4 = Z2Z1 × Z4Z3

3. Reverted Gear Train

  • Special type of compound gear train.
  • Input and output shafts are coaxial.
  • Axes of first and last gears lie on the same straight line.
  • Used where input/output shafts must be aligned (clocks, gearboxes, speed reducers).

4. Epicyclic / Planetary Gear Train

  • Gears whose axes can move relative to the frame.
  • Components: sun gear (central), planet gears (rotate around sun), planet carrier, ring gear (internal teeth).
  • Planet gears rotate about their own axes and revolve around the sun gear.
  • Different speed ratios obtained depending on which component is fixed/driven/output.

Applications & Advantages

  • Marine reduction gear systems.
  • Automatic transmissions.
  • Helicopter transmission systems.
  • Compact construction, high torque capacity.
  • Multiple speed ratios, coaxial input/output possible.
  • Load distributed among several planet gears.

1.6 Gearbox

  • Enclosed mechanical unit containing gears that transmit power at different speeds and torque levels.
  • Used to increase or decrease rotational speed according to machine requirements.
  • Marine reduction gearbox: decreases engine speed to a suitable propeller speed.
  • Since power is approximately conserved, output torque increases when output speed decreases (allowing for losses).
Pin × η = Pout

Where η is transmission efficiency.

Important gearbox components

  • Input shaft, output shaft.
  • Gear wheels, bearings.
  • Gear casing, lubrication system.
  • Oil seals, cooling arrangements.
Common gearbox problems
Gear tooth wearPitting and scoring
Excessive backlashOverheating
Excessive vibrationAbnormal noise
Lubricating oil contamination

2.1 Belt Drives

  • A belt drive uses a flexible belt running over pulleys to transmit power between two or more shafts.
  • Power is transmitted through friction or positive engagement, depending on belt type.
  • Commonly used when shafts are separated by a considerable distance.

Applications

  • Fans and blowers, pumps, compressors.
  • Conveyors, workshop machinery.
  • Cooling and ventilation systems.

2.2 Main Components of Belt Drive

  1. Driving pulley — connected to the power source.
  2. Driven pulley — connected to the machine receiving power.
  3. Belt — flexible element transmitting power.
  4. Tight side — side carrying higher tension.
  5. Slack side — side carrying lower tension.
  6. Centre distance — distance between pulley centres.
  7. Belt tension — tensile force in the belt during operation.

2.3 Types of Belt Drives

TypeDescription
Open beltBoth pulleys rotate in same direction; shafts parallel; belt does not cross.
Crossed beltBelt crosses between pulleys; opposite rotation; larger angle of contact; increased rubbing at crossing.
Quarter-turnShafts at right angles; changes direction of power transmission.
Compound beltMultiple pulleys and belts for large speed ratio.
Stepped / cone pulleyPulleys of different diameters in steps; speed changed by shifting belt.
Fast & loose pulleyFast pulley fixed to shaft (transmits power); loose pulley rotates freely; engage/disengage without stopping driving shaft.

2.4 Types of Belts

BeltFeatures
Flat beltRectangular cross-section; long centre distances; high speed; friction drive.
V-beltTrapezoidal; runs in V-groove; wedge action increases grip; short/medium centre distances; pumps, compressors, motors.
Timing / toothed beltTeeth on inner surface engage pulley grooves; positive drive without slip; accurate timing (engine timing, precision machinery).
Ribbed beltMultiple longitudinal ribs; flexible + good power transmission; compact accessory drives.

2.5 Belt Drive Formulae

For a simple belt drive without slip:

N1D1 = N2D2
N2N1 = D1D2

Remember

  • Larger driven pulley = lower output speed.
  • Smaller driven pulley = higher output speed.

Belt Tension

  • T₁ = tension on tight side.
  • T₂ = tension on slack side.
  • Difference produces effective driving force: F = T₁ − T₂.
P = (T1 − T2)v

Where P = power (W), v = belt velocity (m/s), T₁, T₂ in Newtons.

v = πDN60

Where D is pulley diameter in metres.

Slip in Belt Drives

  • Slip occurs when the belt moves relative to the pulley surface.
  • Reduces actual driven pulley speed.
  • Causes: insufficient friction, inadequate tension, excessive load, worn belt surfaces.
%S = Nideal − NactualNideal × 100

Creep in Belt Drives

  • Small relative movement caused by elastic stretching and contraction.
  • Belt stretches on tight side and contracts on slack side.
  • Causes small reduction in actual speed ratio.
  • Unlike slip, creep occurs due to elastic deformation of the belt material.
SlipCreep
Relative sliding between belt and pulleyRelative movement due to elastic deformation
Caused by insufficient friction or excessive loadCaused by difference in belt tension
Can be considerably reduced by correct tensionCannot be completely eliminated in elastic belts

2.7 Chain Drives

  • Endless chain engages with toothed wheels called sprockets.
  • Power transmitted through positive engagement between chain links and sprocket teeth.
  • No normal slip — suitable where constant speed ratio is required.

Applications

  • Conveyors, hoists, industrial machinery.
  • Engine timing systems, bicycles.
  • Auxiliary equipment.

2.8 Main Components of Chain Drive

  1. Driving sprocket — connected to power source.
  2. Driven sprocket — receives power from chain.
  3. Chain links — interconnected elements forming the chain.
  4. Pin — connects links and allows articulation.
  5. Bush — bearing surface between pin and roller.
  6. Roller — reduces friction between chain and sprocket teeth.
  7. Chain tensioner — maintains appropriate tension.

2.9 Types of Chains

ChainDescription
Roller chainMost common; pins, bushes, rollers, side plates; reliable positive transmission.
Silent chainInverted-tooth; specially shaped toothed links; smoother engagement; less noise.
Leaf chainInterconnected plates and pins; high tensile loading; lifting/hoisting (not sprocket-driven power transmission).
Conveyor chainMoves materials; material handling; can have attachments for products.

2.10 Chain Drive Speed Ratio

N1N2 = Z2Z1

Where N₁ = driving sprocket speed, N₂ = driven sprocket speed, Z₁ = driving teeth, Z₂ = driven teeth. Chain drives provide a positive speed ratio because the chain engages with sprocket teeth.

2.11 Advantages and Disadvantages of Chain Drives

AdvantagesDisadvantages
No normal slipRequires lubrication
Constant speed ratioMore noise than belt drives
High power transmission capabilityRequires accurate alignment
Suitable for moderate centre distancesChain wear causes elongation
More compact than some belt arrangementsRequires periodic tension adjustment
Operates in harsh environmentsVibration and polygonal/chordal action
Polygonal Action: Chain links engage at discrete positions, so the chain does not wrap as a smooth circle. This creates periodic velocity variations. More significant with fewer sprocket teeth.

3.1 Couplings

  • A coupling connects two shafts for transmitting torque and rotational motion.
  • Generally connects driving and driven shafts permanently or semi-permanently.
  • Most couplings remain engaged during normal operation.
  • May accommodate minor misalignment depending on design.

Applications

  • Marine propulsion shafting.
  • Pump and motor connections.
  • Compressor drives, generator sets.
  • Industrial gearboxes.

3.2 Functions of Couplings

  1. Connect two shafts.
  2. Transmit torque and power.
  3. Accommodate minor shaft misalignment (flexible designs).
  4. Reduce transmission of shock and vibration (suitable designs).
  5. Allow convenient assembly and disassembly.
  6. Protect connected equipment (safety couplings).

3.3 Types of Couplings

A. Rigid Couplings

  • Connect shafts without relative flexibility.
  • Require accurate shaft alignment.
TypeDescription
Sleeve / muffHollow cylindrical sleeve over shaft ends; keys transmit torque; simple & economical.
Split muffTwo halves bolted around shaft ends; assembled/removed without moving machinery significantly.
FlangeTwo flanges mounted on shaft ends, bolted together; high torque; marine shafting & industrial drives.

B. Flexible Couplings

  • Allow limited relative movement between shafts.
  • Accommodate small angular, parallel or axial misalignment within limits.
TypeDescription
Bushed pinTwo flanges connected through pins with rubber/resilient bushes; absorbs shock & vibration; pumps & motors.
JawTwo hubs with interlocking jaws; elastomeric spider between jaws; transmits torque & absorbs vibration.
GearExternal gear teeth on hubs, internally toothed sleeves; high torque; limited angular misalignment; requires lubrication.
GridFlexible metallic grid connecting grooved hubs; flexibility & shock absorption.
OldhamTwo hubs + intermediate disc; parallel shafts with small offset; disc slides relative to hubs.

C. Fluid Coupling

  • Hydrodynamic device transmitting power through a fluid.
  • Components: impeller (pump wheel), runner (turbine wheel), enclosed casing, working fluid (oil).
  • Driving impeller rotates → transfers energy to fluid → fluid flows to runner → runner rotates.
  • Power transmitted without direct mechanical contact.
  • Small speed difference (slip) is necessary for torque transmission.

Advantages & Applications

  • Smooth starting, absorbs shock loads.
  • Reduces mechanical shock during acceleration.
  • Useful for high starting loads.
  • Marine propulsion, heavy industrial drives, conveyors.
  • Diesel-driven machinery.

3.4 Coupling Misalignment

TypeDescription
AngularShaft axes meet at an angle.
Parallel / offsetShaft axes parallel but displaced from each other.
AxialShafts displaced along common axis or incorrect axial positioning.

Effects of misalignment

  • Excessive vibration, bearing damage.
  • Coupling wear, increased power consumption.
  • Shaft fatigue, seal failure, abnormal noise.
  • Maintenance: Proper shaft alignment essential after installation, overhaul or foundation work.

3.5 Clutches

  • Mechanical device to engage and disengage power transmission between driving and driven shafts.
  • Allows driving member to rotate while driven member remains stationary or rotates at a different speed during disengagement.
  • Used where controlled starting and stopping is required.

Applications

  • Automobiles, industrial machinery.
  • Marine machinery, machine tools.
  • Power transmission systems.

3.6 Functions of Clutches

  1. Connect and disconnect power transmission.
  2. Allow smooth starting of machinery.
  3. Enable driven shaft to stop without stopping driving shaft.
  4. Allow speed differences during engagement.
  5. Reduce shock during starting when correctly operated.

3.7 Types of Clutches

A. Friction Clutches

  • Transmit torque through friction between contacting surfaces.
  • Engagement by applying pressure between friction plates/surfaces.
TypeDescription
Single plateOne friction plate between flywheel and pressure plate; springs keep engaged; pressing mechanism releases pressure; components: flywheel, clutch plate, pressure plate, springs, release bearing, operating mechanism.
Multi-plateMultiple friction plates alternating between driving and driven members; more friction surfaces → greater torque in compact space; motorcycles & machinery.
ConeConical friction surfaces; high torque for given axial force; less common in modern applications.
CentrifugalOperates automatically based on centrifugal force; shoes move outward as speed increases; used in small engines and automatic machinery.

B. Positive Clutches

  • Transmit power through direct mechanical engagement; do not depend primarily on friction.
  • Dog clutch: specially shaped teeth/dogs engage matching teeth; positive connection; requires speed matching; gearboxes & transmissions.

C. Electromagnetic Clutch

  • Uses electromagnetic force to engage/disengage.
  • Electrical current energises magnetic coil → magnetic force engages clutch members.
  • Used in automated machinery and control systems.

D. Hydraulic Clutch

  • Uses hydraulic pressure to operate the clutch mechanism.
  • Hydraulic fluid transfers force from operating mechanism to clutch actuator.
  • Common in vehicle clutch operating systems.

3.8 Difference Between Coupling and Clutch

FeatureCouplingClutch
Main purposeConnect two shaftsEngage/disengage power
OperationGenerally permanentCan be engaged/disengaged
Power interruptionNot normally possible during operationPossible
MisalignmentFlexible types accommodate limited misalignmentNot its primary purpose
Main useMotor and pump shaftsEngine and transmission
Operation frequencyUsually no frequent engagementFrequent engagement possible
Remember: A coupling connects two shafts; a clutch controls the transmission of power between two rotating members.

4.1 Introduction to Bearings

  • A bearing supports a rotating or moving shaft.
  • Reduces friction between moving parts.
  • Carries loads acting on the shaft.
  • Maintains correct position and alignment of rotating components.

Applications

  • Marine engine crankshafts, propeller shafts.
  • Gearboxes, electric motors.
  • Pumps, compressors, turbines.

4.2 Functions of Bearings

  1. Support rotating shafts.
  2. Reduce friction.
  3. Carry radial and axial loads.
  4. Maintain shaft alignment.
  5. Reduce wear between moving surfaces.
  6. Provide smooth rotation.
  7. Increase machinery service life.

4.3 Classification of Bearings

A. Sliding Contact Bearings

  • Shaft slides over a bearing surface.
  • Lubricant film maintained between shaft and bearing surface.
  • Lubricant reduces friction and prevents metal-to-metal contact under normal conditions.
TypeDescription
Journal bearingSupports rotating shaft with mainly radial loads; shaft rotates inside cylindrical bearing; hydrodynamic lubrication; marine diesel crankshafts, turbines, pumps, compressors.
Footstep / pivotSupports vertical shaft; carries axial load at bottom end; vertical rotating machines.
Thrust bearingCarries axial loads; prevents unwanted axial movement; sliding or rolling elements.
Tilting padMultiple movable pads; each tilts to form stable hydrodynamic film; high-speed heavily loaded machinery.

B. Rolling Contact Bearings

  • Use balls or rollers between inner and outer races.
  • Rolling elements reduce friction compared with sliding contact.
  • Main components: inner race, outer race, rolling elements, cage/retainer.
TypeDescription
Ball bearingSpherical balls; radial and some axial loads; low friction, smooth operation. Types: deep groove, angular contact, self-aligning, thrust.
Cylindrical rollerMainly radial loads; high radial capacity.
Tapered rollerCombined radial and axial loads; wheel and gearbox arrangements.
Spherical rollerHeavy radial loads and some axial; accommodates certain misalignment.
Needle rollerLong thin rollers; where radial space is limited.
Thrust ballAxial loads along shaft axis; not for heavy radial loads.

4.4 Radial Load and Axial Load

LoadDirection / Example
Radial loadPerpendicular to shaft axis; pushes shaft sideways (e.g. belt tension load).
Axial loadParallel to shaft axis; pushes/pulls along length (e.g. propeller thrust).
Combined loadCombination of radial and axial; some bearings specifically designed for both.

4.5 Bearing Failure and Maintenance

ProblemDescription
WearGradual loss of material due to friction and contact.
PittingSmall cavities/pits from repeated contact stresses.
SpallingPieces of bearing material break away from surface.
OverheatingInsufficient lubrication, excessive loading, incorrect clearance, misalignment.
CorrosionSurface damage from moisture or chemical contamination.
FatigueRepeated loading causes material deterioration over time.
MisalignmentImproper alignment causes uneven loading and premature wear.

Signs of bearing failure

  • Abnormal noise, increased vibration.
  • Excessive temperature, increased friction.
  • Metal particles in lubricating oil.
  • Excessive shaft play.

Maintenance practices

  • Check bearing temperature and monitor vibration.
  • Inspect lubrication condition; maintain correct clearances.
  • Avoid overloading; check shaft alignment.
  • Replace damaged bearings.
  • Maintain cleanliness during installation.

4.6 Lubrication

  • Introducing a suitable lubricant between moving surfaces to reduce friction and wear.
  • Lubricant forms a protective film between contacting surfaces.
  • Improves efficiency and reduces heat generation.
  • Common lubricants: mineral oils, synthetic oils, greases, special-purpose fluids.

4.7 Functions of Lubrication

  1. Reduces friction between moving parts.
  2. Minimises wear.
  3. Removes heat from machinery.
  4. Prevents corrosion.
  5. Reduces noise.
  6. Helps remove contaminants and wear particles.
  7. Provides sealing in certain applications.
  8. Extends machinery life.

4.8 Types of Lubrication

TypeDescription
HydrodynamicContinuous lubricant film separates surfaces; relative movement creates pressure supporting load; no metal-to-metal contact under full-film conditions. Journal bearings, turbomachinery.
HydrostaticLubricant supplied under external pressure (pump); film maintained even at zero/low speed; precision machinery, heavily loaded applications.
BoundaryVery thin film; some surface contact may occur; special additives protect surfaces; starting, stopping, insufficient full-film lubrication.
MixedCombination of hydrodynamic and boundary; some load carried by film, some by surface contact; transitional conditions.

4.9 Lubrication Methods

MethodDescription
ManualApplied manually at regular intervals; simple machinery.
Oil bathComponents partially immersed in oil; rotating parts distribute oil.
SplashRotating parts splash oil onto moving components; enclosed gearboxes & engines.
Forced / pressureOil pump supplies lubricant under pressure through passages; marine diesel engines & large machinery.
GreaseGrease supplied to bearing; suitable for many rolling element bearings; some contamination protection.

4.10 Properties of a Good Lubricant

PropertyDescription
ViscosityResistance to flow; determines film thickness & flow. Too low → inadequate film; too high → increased friction & power losses.
Viscosity indexHow much viscosity changes with temperature; higher = less change.
Flash pointLowest temperature at which vapours ignite momentarily under test flame.
Fire pointTemperature at which vapours continue to burn for a specified period.
Pour pointLowest temperature at which oil remains capable of flowing under specified conditions.
Oxidation stabilityResistance to chemical deterioration when exposed to oxygen and heat.
Corrosion protectionAbility to protect metallic surfaces against corrosion.
Thermal stabilityAbility to maintain properties at elevated temperatures.

5.1 Introduction to Cam Mechanisms

  • A cam is a specially shaped rotating or sliding element used to produce a predetermined motion in another component called a follower.
  • Converts rotary or sliding motion into reciprocating or oscillating motion.
  • Follower remains in contact with cam surface through gravity, spring force or positive mechanical constraints.

Applications

  • Internal combustion engine valve mechanisms.
  • Fuel injection pumps.
  • Automatic machinery, textile machinery.
  • Packaging equipment, industrial automation.

5.2 Main Components

  1. Cam — driving element with specially designed profile.
  2. Follower — driven element following the cam contour.
  3. Camshaft — shaft carrying one or more cams.
  4. Spring — maintains contact between cam and follower.
  5. Guide — constrains follower motion along required path.

5.3 Types of Cams

TypeDescription
Radial / discMost common; follower moves radially; cam rotates about fixed axis; engine valve mechanisms.
Cylindrical / barrelGroove cut on rotating cylinder surface; follower moves according to groove; automatic machinery.
WedgeSliding wedge-shaped profile; converts linear sliding into follower movement; specialised arrangements.
ConjugateTwo cams control follower together; maintains positive contact; high-speed precision applications.

5.4 Types of Followers

A. According to Contact Surface

TypeDescription
Knife-edgeSharp edge contacts cam; simple; high friction and wear; rarely used for high loads.
RollerRoller at contact point; rolling contact reduces friction and wear; industrial machinery and engines.
Flat-facedFlat contact surface; carries substantial loads; sliding contact causes friction; requires lubrication.
Spherical-facedCurved contact surface; reduces edge contact and stress concentration; certain engine valve mechanisms.

B. According to Motion

  • Translating / reciprocating — moves in a straight line (vertical, horizontal or inclined).
  • Oscillating — moves through an angular path about a fixed pivot.

C. According to Position

  • Radial / in-line — follower's line of motion passes through cam centre.
  • Offset — follower's line of motion displaced from cam centre; affects motion and pressure angle.

5.5 Important Cam Terminology

TermMeaning
Base circleSmallest circle tangent to cam profile, concentric with cam axis.
Trace pointReference point on follower used to generate cam profile.
Pitch curvePath traced by trace point relative to cam.
Lift / strokeMaximum displacement from lowest to highest follower position.
DwellPeriod follower remains stationary while cam rotates.
FlankPortion of cam profile between base circle and nose.
NosePortion corresponding to maximum follower displacement.
Pressure angleAngle between follower motion direction and normal to pitch curve; excessive → side thrust and friction.

5.6 Cam Motion

A cam mechanism generally has four important motion phases:

  1. Rise (ascent) — follower moves away from lowest position; displacement increases.
  2. Dwell — follower remains stationary; cam continues rotating; used when operation must stay in fixed position.
  3. Return (descent) — follower moves back towards original position.
  4. Dwell again — follower remains at lowest position; cycle repeats.

5.7 Types of Follower Motion

MotionDescription
Uniform velocityConstant velocity during rise/return; sudden velocity changes at start/end cause theoretically infinite acceleration; not suitable for high speed without modification.
Simple harmonic (SHM)Sinusoidal displacement; acceleration and velocity vary smoothly; controlled follower movement.
Uniform acceleration & retardationAccelerates uniformly first half, decelerates uniformly second half; parabolic displacement curve (parabolic motion).
CycloidalCycloidal displacement curve; zero velocity at beginning and end of stroke; smoother than uniform velocity; suitable for relatively high-speed cams.

5.8 Camshaft in Marine Diesel Engines

  • Camshaft operates valves and other mechanisms in many internal combustion engines.
  • Contains several cams mounted at specific angular positions.
  • Each cam controls a particular follower, which transfers motion to the valve or operating component.

Functions in a diesel engine

  • Operates inlet valves (where cam-operated).
  • Operates exhaust valves.
  • Controls valve opening and closing timing.
  • May operate fuel injection pumps in mechanically controlled systems.
  • Maintains correct timing between engine components.

Camshaft maintenance

  • Check cam lobe wear.
  • Inspect follower surfaces.
  • Check valve timing.
  • Ensure correct lubrication.
  • Check bearing clearances.
  • Inspect for cracks and scoring.
  • Check abnormal noise and vibration.
Important: Not all marine diesel engines use the same valve and fuel injection arrangement. Some modern engines use electronic or hydraulic actuation rather than conventional cam-driven systems.
